What’s ‘*Ikigai’* ? A Japanese term meant for principles leading to a long life. Author *Hector Garcia* has wonderfully explored the essence of ‘*Ikigai*’ through his bestselling book *Ikigai: The Japanese Secret to a Long and Happy Life*. Written as 生き甲斐 in Japanese, the word combines 生き which means “life” and 甲斐 meaning “to be worthwhile or beautiful”. Thus, the word’s literal meaning being “a reason for making our life worthwhile or beautiful”. This concept is effectively illustrated by *Mark Win* through a Venn diagram where ‘*Ikigai’* is a result of four overlapping circles: “What I Love”, “What I am Good at”, “What can I be Paid for”, and “What the world Needs”. The intersections of these circles define specific aspects of purpose. “What I love and I am good at” represents my “Passion”. “What I am good at and can I be paid for” defines my “Profession”. “What the world needs and I can be paid for” represents “Vocation” and “What I love and the world needs” defines my “Mission or Goal”.

Although it’s impossible to connect all the dots in my case; it makes me wonder what my ‘*Ikigai’* truly is. Is it my passion, my goal, my academic interests or is it something utterly different, the people I meet, or the relations I have formed over the years or a particular phase of my life. Let’s explore each of these aspects.

Let’s begin by reflecting on my passion. I like music, maybe music is my ‘*Ikigai*’. I’m not a zombie towards music but instead a moody one. My playlist ranges from Chester’s timeless echoes to Arijit and KK’s soulful classics.*”Songs last longer than people! ”* rightfully said indeed. Yet excess music later gives headaches.There will be times when I’m dancing to music, vibin’ and chillin’; and times where I am just irritated by any other ominous tone. So I guess music’s not my ‘*Ikigai*’.

I like gaming. Maybe it’s my ‘*Ikigai’*. I’ve had a childhood full of video games. From those timeless *Miniclip* games, *Max Payne*, *God Of War*, *POP* classics to major releases through my childhood covering Ubisoft, Activision and Sony’s franchises with *Batman* and *Spiderman* as my current favourites. Yet, I face boredom nowadays. Current releases just don’t feel as “relatable”.I guess I’m stuck in a “nostalgic” phase. I’ll cross out gaming from my list of prospective ‘*Ikigai’*.

I am good at academics, maybe they are my ‘*Ikigai*’. I was fascinated by history and literature during my schooling. Of course, being an ICSE kid, I’ve had to bear Shakespeare and his *Merchant of Venice*. That was a great phase. I still remember doodling around in my textbooks and cartoonising figures with cuss words. Then came my JEE phase and with it a new challenge which I eventually enjoyed over the course of time. I’d say it was the best phase of learning I have experienced. I ain’t a Maths guy. Physics and Chemistry always fascinated me more. I’d often ask random, absurd questions, and my mentors answered them happily and guess what? Half of my learning occurred there itself. Solving problems and imagining scenarios refined those concepts even further. Engineering, however, changed my perspective and we all can agree on this. Lost interest in outdated courses pushed me to explore other things. So this ain’t my ‘*Ikigai*’.

I like designing. What began as a necessity became something I embraced. *“Designs and rejections go hand in hand”*, a wise man once said. With conferences, clubs, and fests operating year round, I’ve worked on many projects and experimented with several tools. *Photoshop*, which once felt like “rocket science”, now comes easily to me. Yet, despite the effort, it often feels like unappreciated work in my current environment. Maybe it isn’t my ‘*Ikigai*’ after all. Let’s see other options.

I am an IT grad, “sort of”. I've been good with computers ever since I was a kid. This young fellow could operate a computer and even set cuss words as passwords at the mere age of two. My brother would blindfold me while setting passwords to prevent me from playing his games, yet, I’d still break that stuff. Over the years, I dived deep in this stuff. Usually stuff related to fixing game errors. I’ve known torrenting, fixing registry and DLL errors, exploring BIOS settings, and other such workarounds. Fast forward to the present where I am struggling to do the technical stuff, not because I am unable to do it, but a large part of me is simply fed up with desk work. This ain’t my ‘*Ikigai*’.

I like keychains. Maybe, just maybe, they’re my ‘*Ikigai*’. Keychains have come to hold a special place in my life, for a very special reason indeed. From *Pikachu* and *Shinchan* keychains to pocket watches and other little memorabilia; I’ve had it all once and I’ve lost it all in the blink of an eye. It isn’t keychains then.

I like watches too, maybe my *Black Watch* is my ‘*Ikigai*’. It was gifted to me by someone very special, and I’ve worn it on every important occasion — as if I’m carrying a piece of “Her”. It is my “Lifeline” in a way. Maybe a piece of my heart or a piece that fills it. Maybe my black watch is indeed my ‘*Ikigai*’. Still, let's explore other aspects.

Is it entertainment media? I like cartoons and anime, maybe they are my ‘*Ikigai*’, especially *Shinchan* and *Pikachu*. From having a childhood full of watching cartoons such as *KochiKame*, *DBZ*, *Beyblade*, and other classics and watching the same *Shinchan* movie thrice a day at a time to now just scrolling through reels as “nostalgia”. Coming to anime, Deathnote was my first anime and I’d love rewatching it. Later on, I’ve binge watched major anime like *JJK*, *AOT*, but  still I’d prefer shorter ones nowadays if I have to watch afresh. Maybe it's movies and series? I’ve watched them too but still they’re a good refresher but not a constant escape for me. I’d rather have a binge watch partner for this stuff. It’s not entertainment media then.

Maybe money’s my ‘*Ikigai*’. Is it? From what I’ve experienced, earning money isn’t the sole purpose. You can have a lotta money but still be unable to utilize it. You can earn a lotta money and still stay unappreciated. There are reasons but I guess let’s just say I’m stuck between a rock and a hard place. Money ain’t my ‘*Ikigai*’.

Maybe my ‘*Ikigai*’ isn’t materialistic. Let’s look at the people in my life. Starting with my parents. Are my parents my ‘*Ikigai*’? If I were asked this a few years ago, I’d definitely say yes. But things have changed. They provided everything I needed, from a wonderful childhood full of key moments to a great career path. Truly, I was born with a silver spoon. Things fell apart, though, when I reached my teenage years and had to “decide” my life path. “Decide”? Was I given a choice in “important decisions” or was I even properly appreciated ? A good career, I think, is one where passion meets skills that are in demand, and progress is followed by appreciation for reaching milestones, which wasn’t the case for me. I can go on a long rant about this but that’s a story for another day. They aren’t my ‘*Ikigai*’.

Maybe my brother? Like I said, my childhood was awesome. He’s a supportive and loving comrade. I remember riding his bike when he learned to drive, going on outings with him in my teenage years, fighting with him and getting beatings in childhood. He’s a good advisor too although our communication has decreased as we aged but we still find time to enjoy each other’s company. Yet, there are a few things that distance him from what I believe ‘*Ikigai*’ truly means.

My grandparents? On my paternal side, there isn’t a strong bond, actually. I actually hated them since childhood but later on during teenage years things started to cool down. My maternal grandparents, on the other hand, formed a close bond. I’d still remember our trips to *Nareshwar* and *Garudeshwar*, or going on outings with my *Appa*.The last token he gave me was a steel bottle, one I still use this day. Maybe, he is my ‘*Ikigai'*.

How are my friends and people around me, overall? I am not a typical extrovert with dozens of friends or relatives. I can form friendships, but I’m unable to preserve them. Events occur. People change. Things just go out of hand. But I’ve observed that, wherever I go, I do form a bond. School life flashed by. Coaching life began with groups, chats and texts which were followed by meaningful relations with a few of them. Even my mentors were great and so is my bond. Sometimes it feels nostalgic and fun to revisit old faces, catch up, and reimagine myself in the past. It’s *‘Deja Vu’* literally. College life, though, didn’t start as I expected. Here too, we have a pack, but there are problems within. But I’ve been able to open up to them easily, at least to some extent. I may be a shy guy or a troublemaker and wreak havoc. I’ve started and lost fights, irritated people, pulled chains, ruined a date, experienced gpls and documented memories although small. I guess I’ve experienced though not as one would like to during his college life. Maybe these people are my ‘*Ikigai*’. 

What about *‘Her’*? Is ‘*She*’ my ‘*Ikigai*’? I’ve experienced it all — the butterflies, the thrill, the fun — all in the wrong place and at the wrong time. I still remember waiting the whole day just to experience a fleeting moment. I still remember the girl in pink, excited about my exam score and running towards me to congratulate.The same girl in pink who gifted me my black watch before running away. The same girl who shared ‘*Her’* endless foolish late-night talks which I loved listening to and reading. The same girl made me realise that pictures mean a lot in the long run. They form memories, which we revisit and share. The same girl constantly reminded me that the present and the future matter more than the past. All good things start with a childish letter, a keychain, and excitement that later shapes into a strong *‘Bond’* which we deem unbreakable. Complications arise, yet we try to preserve the *‘Bond’*. Discussions and clashes occur, and career importance overshadows some wishes. A long wait begins, and with it, life’s hurdles increase. *“Belive in God’s Plan”*, as *‘She’* said, which involved hard work for earning what you’re entitled for. ‘*She’* worked for a goal and got what *‘She’* had wished for. Meanwhile, I supported ‘*Her’* in my way and tried appreciating her in every way possible, for ‘*She’* had brought the brightest smile and heartiest tears with this deed, which even my hard work couldn’t. Things are complicated, though, as I am labelled *“Right Person, Wrong Place, Wrong Time”* as a bittersweet conclusion to a chapter and the *‘Bond’* I still cling to. I guess *‘She’* is still my *“Ikigai”*.

What’s my ‘*Ikigai’* then? There’s no specific answer. There are strong reasons to believe it is *‘Her’,* and the *‘Bond’* we share. And then there are the other things that I spoke of — the things I do or the way I interact. I don't know why. I guess the answers are already upfront, or maybe there are still a lot of loose ends. The phase I am in is perfect for being lost as important is finding a purpose. I guess I’m just a man lost in his twenties trying to figure it out.
